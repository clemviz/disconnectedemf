Five habits that cut your night-time EMF exposure

18 August 2026 · 6 min read Practical tips Sleep

The bedroom is where the body repairs itself, and it is also where most people are exposed for the longest uninterrupted stretch of their day. That combination makes it the first room worth dealing with, and the cheapest to improve.

1. Move the bed away from the wiring

The headboard wall is often the busiest wall in the house: sockets, switches, a ring main running at head height. Electric fields from live wiring fall away quickly with distance, so shifting a bed by fifty centimetres, or moving it to a different wall, frequently halves the reading at the pillow. This costs nothing.

2. Put the router on a timer

A mechanical plug-in timer that cuts the router between midnight and six in the morning removes hours of radiofrequency exposure every night, for the price of a coffee. Nobody in the house notices. If you work late, set it to switch off an hour after you normally stop.

3. Replace the DECT base, not just the handset

Cordless phone bases transmit continuously in many models, whether or not a call is in progress, and they are often in the hallway next to a bedroom wall. Either choose an eco-mode base that stops transmitting at rest, or go back to a corded handset in the room where the phone actually gets used.

4. Get chargers and extension leads off the bedside table

Phone chargers, powered lamps and extension blocks sit centimetres from your head for eight hours a night. Move them to the floor on the far side of the room, and charge the phone outside the bedroom entirely, which also removes its own transmissions from the equation.

5. Switch the phone to flight mode, and mean it

Flight mode stops the phone searching for a mast, which is the behaviour that drives its output highest: a phone with a weak signal transmits harder than one sitting on full bars. If you use it as an alarm clock, flight mode keeps the alarm and drops the transmission.

How far do these five get you?

In most of the bedrooms we measure, these changes alone bring night-time readings down substantially, often enough that no shielding material is needed at all. An assessment tells you which of the five matters most in your specific room, and what is left afterwards.
