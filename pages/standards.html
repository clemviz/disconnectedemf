The standards we report against

National standards do not consider the biological impact of electromagnetic fields. That is why Disconnected relies on standards derived from independent scientific work: Bio-Initiative, SBM-2024 and IGNIR.

Why independent standards

Official exposure limits are built around the Specific Absorption Rate, which describes how much energy body tissue absorbs as heat. Stay below the limit and you will not be cooked. That is genuinely all the limit promises.

The effects documented in the independent literature are largely non-thermal: sleep, oxidative stress, immune response, DNA repair. Because they are not heating effects, a heat-based limit says nothing useful about them. Reporting against biology-based thresholds is the precautionary approach, and it is the one we take.

2012

Bio-Initiative

The Bio-Initiative Report (2012) was prepared by twenty-nine authors from ten countries: ten holding medical degrees, twenty-one PhDs, and three with MSc, MA or MPH degrees. It is the result of a meta-analysis of a large body of studies.

The report concludes that official standards are unsuitable, and even dangerous, for our health. Bio-Initiative redefined threshold values intended to preserve the integrity of human health. Disconnected refers to those threshold values in its assessments, by precautionary principle.

2015

SBM (Standard of Building Biology Testing Methods)

The Institute of Building Biology in Germany, which brings together leading experts on both low and high frequencies, created the SBM-2015 standard. It is the most demanding quality standard on the subject of electromagnetic wave exposure. It was updated in 2024 to SBM-2024, which is the version Disconnected uses for its reports.

The health department of the Salzburg region in Austria officially adopted these values for mobile telephony. According to the underlying work, the first signs attributable to electromagnetic exposure (insomnia, fatigue, difficulty recovering) appear beyond the threshold values determined by SBM-2015.

More about SBM

2017

IGNIR: International Guidelines on Non-Ionizing Radiation

IGNIR is a set of guidelines based on the EUROPAEM EMF Guidelines 2016. The standard was born in October 2017 in Oxford, in the United Kingdom. The guidelines have been updated and expanded in Jan-2021

The guidelines are the outcome of an independent multidisciplinary team including medical doctors, scientists, and representatives of vulnerable and sensitive groups.
